## How Media Influence Political Behavior

[WRITE: Several paragraphs (this is one of the four required sections).
Cover at least these mechanisms — explain each with an example:
  - **Agenda-setting** — outlets decide which stories are "news" and which
    aren't, shaping what voters even think about.
  - **Framing** — the angle a story takes (e.g., "tax cut" vs. "loss of
    public revenue") changes how readers feel about the same fact.
  - **Selection and omission** — which quotes, images, and statistics are
    included or left out.
  - **Repetition** — hearing the same talking point across many outlets
    makes it feel true ("illusory truth effect").
  - **Emotional appeals** — outrage and fear drive clicks and shares more
    than calm analysis.
  - **Algorithmic feeds** — social platforms show you content that keeps
    you scrolling, which usually means content you already agree with
    (filter bubbles / echo chambers).
End with a sentence transitioning into the next section.]

## Every Medium Has Its Own Bias

Different formats shape information in different ways. The medium itself —
not just the outlet — influences how you receive a message.

### Television News

[WRITE: 2–4 sentences on TV's specific tendencies — visuals dominate over
substance, time pressure forces oversimplification, "breaking news" framing
creates urgency, talking-head panels reward conflict over consensus. Give an
example.]

### Radio and Podcasts

[WRITE: 2–4 sentences. Talk radio and many podcasts are built around a host's
personality, which encourages a single perspective for long stretches. No
visuals means tone of voice does a lot of persuasive work. Long-form formats
can go deep but can also normalize fringe ideas through sheer airtime.]

### Online Articles and News Websites

[WRITE: 2–4 sentences. Headlines are written for clicks, not accuracy. Many
sites blend reporting and opinion on the same page. Native ads and "sponsored
content" can look like real journalism.]

### Social Media

[WRITE: 2–4 sentences. Algorithms reward engagement (often outrage), not
accuracy. Posts are stripped of context. Anyone can publish, so credentials
and sourcing are often invisible. Short videos compress complex issues into
seconds. Influencers blur the line between entertainment and reporting.]
